## Daily Insight: Why Agentic AI Is Rewriting the T&E Prepayment Audit Playbook

Prepayment audits have traditionally been a bottleneck in the expense lifecycle: a compliance team samples a fraction of submitted reports, manually cross-references receipts against policy limits, and flags anomalies days or weeks after the spend has already occurred. Agentic AI changes this equation by moving audit logic from a periodic, sample-based check into a continuous, transaction-level process. Rather than a static rules engine that flags a report for exceeding a dollar threshold, an agentic system can reason across multiple signals simultaneously — merchant category, itinerary context, historical spending patterns for that employee or cost center, and even the semantic content of a receipt image — before payment is ever issued. This shift from "review after the fact" to "verify before the fact" is what makes agentic AI fundamentally different from the rules-based expense automation that preceded it.

The practical impact for finance teams is a reduction in both false positives and missed fraud. Legacy systems tend to either over-flag (burying auditors in low-risk exceptions) or under-flag (missing sophisticated policy circumvention, such as split transactions or altered receipts). Agentic AI auditors can chain together multiple steps autonomously — pulling a traveler's itinerary from a booking tool, comparing it against the expense claim, checking for duplicate submissions across expense and corporate card feeds, and only escalating to a human when genuine ambiguity exists. This narrows the human review queue to the cases that actually require judgment, which is where skilled auditors add the most value. For travel teams specifically, this also means faster reimbursement cycles, since compliant claims can clear prepayment review automatically rather than waiting in a manual queue.

Looking ahead, the maturity curve for these systems will likely be measured less by raw detection accuracy and more by auditability and trust. A finance organization adopting an agentic auditor needs a clear record of what the agent checked, what data it used, and why it approved or escalated a given claim — especially for SOX and internal-controls purposes. The T&E vendors gaining traction in 2026 are the ones pairing autonomous decisioning with transparent, replayable audit trails, not just faster fraud detection. As agentic AI becomes a standard layer in the prepayment stack, the differentiator for CFOs evaluating vendors will shift from "does it catch fraud" to "can I explain and defend every decision it made."
